## 5-minute review

- Human rights, fundamental rights and citizenship rights are related but not interchangeable terms.

- The EU Charter and the ECHR belong to different institutional systems.

- Article 35 concerns access to preventive care and medical treatment, with national conditions.

- AAAQ means availability, accessibility, acceptability and quality.

- Accessibility includes financial, physical, information and non-discrimination dimensions.

- Respect, protect and fulfil identify different state obligations.

- Legal permission for a vaccination policy is not the same question as whether it should be chosen.

## Which document and which court?


The lecturer distinguishes citizenship rights tied to nationality, fundamental rights by their foundational importance, and human rights held by human beings. The EU, the Council of Europe and the UN are not interchangeable sources. Name the document before interpreting the right.

The lecture pairs the EU Charter with the Court of Justice in Luxembourg and the European Convention on Human Rights with the European Court of Human Rights in Strasbourg. It notes similarities in interpretation without merging the two systems. An answer that cites Article 8 ECHR should therefore not attribute that provision to the EU Charter.

## From a broad idea of health to a usable framework


The WHO material in the lecture treats health more broadly than absence of disease. General Comment 14 provides a framework for examining the services and conditions needed to realise the right. Availability asks whether sufficient functioning provision exists. Accessibility asks whether people can reach and use it without discriminatory, geographical, financial or information barriers.

Acceptability asks whether care respects ethics and cultural contexts. Quality asks whether services are scientifically and medically appropriate. These are complementary checks, not a league table. A well-equipped clinic can still be inaccessible, while a nearby affordable service can still have a quality problem.

## Who must do what?


The slides move beyond a simple positive-versus-negative rights split. Respect concerns avoiding interference with enjoyment of the right. Protect concerns preventing interference by third parties. Fulfil concerns the legislative, administrative, budgetary and other measures needed to make the right effective.

Article 35 is presented as a healthcare entitlement connected to national law and practice, not an unlimited guarantee of any desired treatment. Supplementary scope note: the Commission’s public explanation says that national authorities are bound by the Charter when implementing EU law. A study answer should state the scope question rather than imply that Article 35 decides every domestic dispute by itself.

## Vavřička: keep the reasoning and the facts together


The lecture’s analysis follows scope, interference and justification under Article 8 ECHR. Justification involves a legal basis, a legitimate aim and necessity in a democratic society, including proportionality. It discusses protection of vulnerable children and solidarity alongside individual physical integrity.

The safeguards and circumstances matter: no vaccine was administered against the applicants’ will, exemptions and review procedures were relevant, and the sanctions were examined in context. The case is a structured example concerning childhood vaccination, not a blanket answer for every disease, population or mandate. The later discussion deliberately changes the question from whether a policy can be mandatory to whether it should be.
