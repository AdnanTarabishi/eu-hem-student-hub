## Why end with a workshop?


The workshop revisits the historical development of European health action and asks what the right to health means for current and future challenges. Its course-title diagram asks which groups have needs, which resources are involved, where they should be provided and by which level.

The diagram includes more than money: it also asks about analogue and digital resources, vulnerable groups, social and commercial determinants, and the relationship between health and other policies. It is an agenda for analysis, not an invitation to assume all priorities have already been settled.

## An umbrella of initiatives


The deck groups cross-border health threats, institutional roles, cancer, pharmaceuticals, health data, mental health and global health under the EHU discussion. Its timelines contain both enacted measures and proposals from different periods.

When using a timeline entry in an answer, identify whether it is a proposal, an adopted instrument or an implementation milestone. A policy initiative’s presence in the diagram is not evidence that every part was operational at the date of the class. This edition does not silently update historical slides into a current legislative tracker.

## Read the two preparation documents differently


The lecturer assigns the 2006 Council conclusions and the European Health Union manifesto. The conclusions articulate common health-system values and operating principles while recognising different national arrangements. The manifesto is a document advocating a direction for a Health Union.

Critical reading does not require agreement with every proposal or a signature. Ask what each document says should be protected, what institutional change it implies, and what trade-offs it leaves unresolved. The Hub’s workshop sheet is an original preparation aid, not the lecturer’s assessment rubric.

## Existing framework versus proposed future


The final slides present four proposals: reframe the values around a Health Union, add a purpose-driven preamble, articulate a cumulative set of core values, and connect those values to action-guiding principles. Their proposed set includes dignity, freedom and autonomy, universal access, equity, resilience and solidarity.

The deck attributes these proposals to a 2026 publication. This study edition identifies them as proposals reported in the deck, without claiming independent review of the cited article or that the values have been adopted as new binding law. Use the workshop builder to specify a population, a problem, a value, practical action, a trade-off and an accountable actor.
