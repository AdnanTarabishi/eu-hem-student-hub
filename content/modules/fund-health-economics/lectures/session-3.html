01 / Change the question

From “How much care?” to “How much health?”

Session 3 takes the demand curve from Session 2 one level deeper. Healthcare is an input; health is the outcome people ultimately value.

H

Health is a stock

Past and present choices shape a state of health that persists and produces services over time.

t

Health creates time

Better health means fewer days lost to illness and more time available for work, home production and other activities.

I

Health can be produced

Individuals combine their own time with purchased healthcare inputs to influence health.

Influence is not control. The lecture keeps genetics, environment and chance alongside individual choices.

03 / Three roles

Health does three jobs in the Grossman model.

CAPITAL

A stock across time

Health today affects what is available tomorrow.

CONSUMPTION

Something people value

Health itself can contribute directly to utility.

PRODUCTION

A source of productive time

Better health reduces time lost to illness and expands what can be done.

04 / Single-period choice

Health H and home good Z do not always trade off.

The Grossman PPF has a distinctive shape because health affects the amount of productive time available.

+

Free-lunch zone

At very low health, a small improvement can reduce sick time so much that both health and home production rise together.

↔

Trade-off zone

Once the large time gains are exhausted, further health improvement uses resources that could have produced Z.

Someone who values both H and Z normally chooses on the downward-sloping trade-off part of the PPF, not in the free-lunch zone, because moving toward the peak can increase both goods.

06 / Across periods

Health depreciates. Investment repairs it.

Ht = (1 − δt)Ht−1 + It−1

Inherited health survives only partly because depreciation removes a fraction δ. Gross investment I refills the stock.

Net investment = It−1 − δtHt−1

I > δH

Net investment is positive and health stock rises.

I = δH

Gross investment exactly replaces deterioration and health is maintained.

I < δH

Net investment is negative and health stock falls.

08 / Returns to health capital

The MEC asks whether another unit of H is worth it.

MEC

Marginal efficiency of capital

The return to another unit of health capital. The lecture places the highest returns at low health and lower returns at high health.

r+δ

User cost of health capital

Resources invested in health have an alternative financial return r, while health also depreciates at rate δ.

Optimal H* where marginal return ≈ r + δ

12 / Two-minute review

The Grossman model in six steps.

1 · Health is a stock

It persists, depreciates and provides a flow of healthy time.

2 · People can invest in it

Time and healthcare produce health, but genetics, environment and chance remain.

3 · Health can be consumption and investment

It may give direct utility and also create productive time.

4 · The PPF has a free-lunch zone

At low health, better health can increase productive time enough to raise both H and Z.

5 · Across time, depreciation matters

H_t = (1−δ)H_{t−1}+I_{t−1}.

6 · Equilibrium compares return with cost

The MEC is balanced against r+δ, then age, wage, education and medical prices move the equilibrium.
