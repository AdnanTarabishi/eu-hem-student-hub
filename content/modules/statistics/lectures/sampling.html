03 / Make the distinction

Individuals vary. Averages vary too.

A different random sample usually gives a different answer. That variation is part of sampling.

σ

Standard deviation

Describes the spread of individual observations in the population. Some children weigh much more or less than others.

σ = 12.99 kg

Measuring more children does not, by itself, change the population’s spread.

CLASS 5 · STANDARD DEVIATION AND STANDARD ERROR
SE

Standard error

Describes the spread of sample means across repeated samples. It tells you about the precision of your estimator.

SE(X̄) = σ / √n

With n = 100, SE = 12.99 / 10 = 1.299 kg. Both SD and SE use kg, not kg².

04 / The theory you need

Why the sample mean works.

Unbiased: centered on the right target

E[X̄] = μ. Across repeated random samples, the average sample mean equals the population mean. A particular sample can still miss the target.

Consistent: more data brings greater precision

As sample size increases, the sample mean converges in probability to μ under the lecture’s i.i.d., finite-variance assumptions. Unbiasedness and consistency are different properties.

Sampling error is different from selection bias

Sampling error is chance variation from observing only a sample. Selection bias systematically favors some people. A larger sample does not fix a biased selection process.

When does the normal distribution enter?

If the population is normal, the sample mean is normal for any n, under independent sampling.

For i.i.d. observations with finite variance, the central limit theorem makes the standardized sample mean approximately normal as n grows.

The lecture uses n ≥ 30 as a rule of thumb. Strong skewness or extreme tails can require a larger sample; 30 is not a universal guarantee.

EXERCISE 5.1

A tiny population, many possible samples.

Five people weigh 65, 89, 75, 64, and 86 kg.

μ = 379 / 5 = 75.8 kg

For samples of four, the five possible means are 78.5, 72.5, 76, 78.75, and 73.25 kg. Their average is 75.8 kg: an illustration of unbiasedness.

Sampling without replacement from this tiny population needs a finite-population correction for the standard error. The lab shows the exact distribution instead.

EXERCISE 5.2

Individual weights are uniform. Means are approximately normal.

Population mean = 27.5 kg; variance = 168.75 kg²; standard deviation ≈ 12.99 kg. Take n = 100 independent observations.

E[X̄] = 27.5 kg
SE(X̄) ≈ 1.299 kg

The sample mean is approximately normal even though individual weights are uniform. Try changing n in the sampling lab.

A range for the unknown mean.

CONFIDENCE INTERVALS · CLASS 5

The full lecture derives confidence intervals from the sampling distribution of the mean. A sample-based interval expresses uncertainty around the estimated population mean.

95% CI = x̄ ± 1.96 × σ / √n

Use this z interval when the population standard deviation is known and the sampling distribution is normal or approximately normal. For an observed mean of 70 kg, σ = 20 kg, and n = 100, the interval is 66.08 to 73.92 kg.

Interpret it carefully. In repeated sampling, about 95% of intervals made by this method contain the fixed population mean. This is not a range containing 95% of individual weights, or a 95% probability assigned to μ after this particular interval is observed.

If population SD is unknown, the usual normal-population method uses sample SD and a t critical value instead. That calculation is outside this page’s known-σ calculator.
