## Italy — payment units, regional choices and practical access

The lecture starts with largely public financing and regional organisation. Its
payment discussion distinguishes GP capitation, hospital DRGs and pharmaceutical
negotiation. The capitation table demonstrates different margins across patient
cost groups; it does not prove actual risk selection. The DRG slides contrast
possible efficiency gains with strategic behaviour, innovation and quality concerns.

The regional diagrams connect national LEA with regional organisation and fiscal
resources. The three ASL types are kept separate: third payer, sponsor and programmer.
The programmer's indirect reimbursement is not automatic. Patient mobility is
primarily interregional Italian movement, not automatically international EU care.
The two study summaries retain their geographical/procedural bounds. The synthesis
connects solidarity, payment incentives and access without claiming one uniform
regional model or one established causal explanation for all differences.

Editorial cautions:

- Spending charts use different units, years and denominators. Page 6's private
  spending chart has a truncated vertical axis and is not total national spending.
- Page 18 tax rates are source descriptions, not independently verified current tax
  rules. Page 21 equipment units/year are unclear, so no per-population ranking is made.
- Pages 28–29 waiting figures lack a demonstrated common denominator/time basis;
  they cannot be silently reconciled into an improvement trend.
- The EUR5.04 billion figure on page 30 is explicitly for 2022 Italian interregional
  mobility. Page 32's amounts/percentages have insufficiently clear common scope;
  no combined ratio is calculated.
- Berta et al. (2022) and Martini et al. (2021) remain lecturer summaries. The latter
  concerns hip replacement in Lombardy, Veneto and Emilia-Romagna, not all care.
- The final fiscal-residuum table cites Ferrario and Zanardi (2009). It is historical.
  Questions without supplied answers remain open rather than being filled in as
  the lecturer's answer.

## Austria — foundations, path dependency and transformation

The guide preserves the narrative of historical foundations, institutional
resilience, governance and reform. It explains contribution-based solidarity,
corporatist self-administration and occupational grouping alongside the deck's
later acknowledgement of taxes. The cross-border examples illustrate specific
labour, tariff and coordination barriers; their current project details are not
independently verified.

The 2020 merger is separated from savings and harmonisation outcomes. The federal,
Länder and social-insurance responsibilities form a coordination problem across
budgets. Wait-or-pay connects broad coverage to practical access, and the 80%
example applies to a contracted reference rate, not any private invoice. The
proposed digital–ambulatory–inpatient sequence remains a strategy/target, not a
completed reform or a clinical rule for an individual patient.

Editorial cautions:

- Page 19's listed revenue shares sum to100.5%. They are not silently normalised.
- Page 21's EU spending comparator of EUR1,001 conflicts with the page 22 chart's
  EUR3,832 in EUR PPP for2023. This disagreement is visible in the guide. No recall
  question treats the disputed comparator as settled.
- Page 21 mixes euro/dollar-PPP and bed figures without a demonstrably compatible
  basis. No ratio or current league table is inferred.
- Figure publication dates differ from underlying observation years. Population
  coverage, unmet need and public financing shares are different measures.
- Page 24 combines annual and five-year funding. No total is made without aligning
  periods and checking overlap. A budget allocation is not proof of an outcome.
- Court of Audit and project accounts are attributed to the lecture, not presented
  as fresh primary-source verification.

## Norway — responsibilities, financing and coordination

The actual deck extends beyond its short opening outline. It covers institutions,
specialist services, municipal care, policy initiatives, comparisons and challenges.
The guide follows these blocks, separating funds, services and referrals.

The somatic-grant illustration is70% need-adjusted /30% activity-based, with research
grants separate. The GP50/50 method split and50/20/30 payer split are treated as two
views of the same simplified income; they are not added together and do not describe
national household expenditure. Municipal care covers varied users, not only older
people; its stairway illustrates settings rather than a mandatory clinical route.

Waiting indicators, individual guarantees and pathway intervals are distinct.
Discharge and acute-unit reforms are evaluated using capacity, readmission, mortality
and group/staffing differences, not only shorter hospital stays. International charts
retain uncertainty, dates and regional coverage. Future challenges include both ageing
and improved function, workforce shortages and demand from younger users.

Editorial cautions:

- Several slides retain2024 footers; others say22 October2026. The filename does
  not update every observation. Municipality/county counts are not verified current
  administrative facts and are not tested as such.
- The user-fee graph supplies no actual current cap or complete eligibility rules.
  The lab explicitly uses invented units and assumes all entered charges qualify.
- Cancer-pathway times are an educational source example, not current clinical advice.
- Discharge-payment rates on page52 are dated2012/2024; no2026 fee is inferred.
- Early comparative charts have unclear dates; later historical mortality charts
  use2009–2014 data and confidence intervals. Italian observations cover four
  provinces of Friuli-Venezia-Giulia, not the entire country.
- Input/outcome association does not establish the causal superiority of a model.
