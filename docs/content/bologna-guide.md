EU-HEM starts in Bologna: Semester 1 is taught here for everyone, at the Department of Economics [S1] (see "Who studies here" above). The centre is compact and walkable, and famous for its **porticoes**, the covered walkways along most streets.

The most urgent steps on arrival: non-EU students apply for the **residence permit within 8 days** [S2], and everyone needs an Italian **tax code** (*codice fiscale*) [S6].

## 2. Before you move

### 3–4 months before
- **Housing:** register with **SAIS**, the free housing service for international students, before you arrive. SAIS also gives non-EU students the housing certificate needed for the entry visa [S7].
- **ER.GO residences** (subsidised student rooms) are assigned through a call that is open to students enrolling in degree programmes, not to exchange students [S7].
- **Non-EU students:** you need a study visa. Follow the instructions from EU-HEM and UniBo for the visa process.

### 1–2 months before
- **Health cover:** EU students bring the EHIC (European Health Insurance Card). Non-EU students need health insurance to get the residence permit: either registration with the Italian National Health Service (SSN) or private insurance [S3].
- **Tax code:** non-EU students can ask for it at the Italian embassy or consulate (if it offers the service) or, free, through UniBo's **IWD** (International Welcome Desk) app [S6].
- **Money for the first weeks:** the residence permit application costs about **€116.46** at the post office [S2], and SSN registration at least **€700** a year [S3].

### First two weeks
- **Non-EU students:** apply for the residence permit **within 8 days** of arriving (see section 3) [S2].
- **Tax code** if you don't have it yet [S6].
- **Bus:** buy the subsidised UniBo annual pass in Studenti Online (see section 6) [S10].
- **University accounts:** Studenti Online, Virtuale, your institutional email and your student badge.
- **EU citizens staying more than 3 months:** register at the city registry office (*Anagrafe*). Documents and booking: **not verified**. Check the official page: [Comune di Bologna](https://www.comune.bologna.it/).

### Coming back to Bologna for Semester 3
For students whose track brings them back to Bologna after Semester 2 abroad (see "Who studies here" above).
- **Residence permit (non-EU):** a student permit lasts at most **12 months** and never longer than your health cover. Renewal must be requested **at least 60 days before it expires**, with renewed SSN registration or insurance [S2][S3]. Check the expiry date **before you leave for Semester 2**. Whether you can renew from abroad, or need a new visa to come back: **not verified**. Ask UniBo's international office and EU-HEM before you leave.
- **Health cover (non-EU, SSN):** registration runs per calendar year, **1 January to 31 December**, with no reduced fee for shorter periods. You can pay the current and the next year together to stay covered [S3]. A return in autumn falls in a new calendar year, so plan the new payment.
- **Housing:** you will need a room again. Register with SAIS and check UniBo's housing services before you come back [S7]. ER.GO's benefits call is published in July [S15].
- **Bus pass:** the subsidised annual pass is bought each academic year in Studenti Online, with a deadline in December [S10].
- **Tax code:** keep your tax-code certificate; you will need it again for a new rental contract and the SSN [S6].
- **City registration (EU citizens):** whether you must register again after time abroad: **not verified**. Ask the Anagrafe.

## 3. Residence and registration

### EU/EEA students
- You don't need a residence permit.
- If you stay **more than 3 months**, register at the city registry office (*Anagrafe*). Required documents for students and how to book: **not verified**. Check the official page: [Comune di Bologna](https://www.comune.bologna.it/).
- **Tax code:** EU students book an appointment in person at the Italian Revenue Agency (*Agenzia delle Entrate*) [S6].

### Non-EU students
- **Residence permit (*permesso di soggiorno*):** apply **within 8 days** of arriving in Italy [S2].
- Get the application kit at a post office with a **"Sportello Amico"** counter, or from an immigration service centre [S2].
- At the post office you pay **€30.46 + €40.00** for the permit, **€30.00** for insured mail and a **€16.00** revenue stamp: about **€116.46** in total [S2].
- The post office gives you an **appointment at the Questura** (central police station, immigration office) for your personal and biometric data [S2].
- The permit lasts at most **12 months** and never longer than your health insurance [S2][S3].
- **Renewal:** apply **at least 60 days before** it expires, with renewed health insurance or SSN registration [S2].
- **Tax code:** non-EU students can't request it at the Revenue Agency. Get it from the Italian embassy or consulate, at the Questura during the fingerprint appointment, or free through UniBo's **IWD** app [S6].

## 4. Healthcare

### EU/EEA students
- With a valid **EHIC** you can go directly to a doctor or a public health facility and show the card. You may pay a small non-refundable contribution (*ticket*) [S3].
- You can choose a general practitioner (GP) from the list of affiliated doctors [S3].
- No EHIC: take private insurance or register voluntarily with the SSN [S3].

### Non-EU students
| Option | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **SSN registration** (Italian National Health Service) | At least **€700 a year** [S3] | Runs per calendar year (1 Jan–31 Dec), no reduced fee for shorter periods; you can pay two years together. Gives you a GP [S3]. |
| **Private insurance** | Varies | Must cover illness, injury and pregnancy in Italy. You usually pay first and claim a refund; no GP [S3]. |

- **To activate the SSN:** pay with the F24 form, then go to an AUSL CUP office with your passport, tax code, enrolment certificate, payment receipts and residence-permit documents. Paying alone does not activate your cover [S3].

### For all students
- **GP while you study:** students from outside Bologna who are in the SSN can register with a GP for the duration of their studies [S4].
- **Healthcare expenses fund:** UniBo has given contributions of **€800** (non-EU students) and **€300** (EU students) towards healthcare costs; the last call closed on 31 December 2025 [S4]. Check whether there is a new call.
- **Out-of-hours doctor (*guardia medica*):** **051 3131**, weekday nights 20:00–8:00 and weekends/holidays from 10:00 to Monday 8:00 [S4].
- **Psychological support (SAP):** free counselling for UniBo students; request an appointment by email to sap@unibo.it with the two forms on the SAP page [S5].
- **Pharmacies** (*farmacia*, green cross sign) can advise on minor problems.

## 5. Housing

- **What it costs:** in August 2026 the average room in Bologna cost about **€585 a month**, 7.5% less than a year before (Immobiliare.it data reported by Il Giorno) [S8]. UniBo's own page still says €400 for a single room and €300 for a shared room, but it has no date and other prices on it are out of date [S9].
- **ER.GO residences:** subsidised rooms through a yearly call, open to students enrolling in degree programmes [S7].
- **SAIS:** free housing help for international students [S7].
- **Vetrina Alloggi** (Accommodation Showcase): private rentals whose details UniBo and ER.GO check [S7].
- **Rent subsidy:** UniBo has offered **€1,000** grants towards rent for students from outside Bologna (600 grants for 2025/26) [S7]. Check whether there is a new call.
- **Register the contract:** tenancy agreements must be registered with the Revenue Agency; non-EU students need this for the residence permit [S7].
- **Websites:** Idealista, Immobiliare.it, Coinquilino, HousingAnywhere, Uniplaces, Spotahome; also student Facebook and Telegram groups, and older EU-HEM students.
- **Neighbourhoods** (general advice, not from an official source): the university area and the centre are walkable and lively but more expensive and can be noisy; San Donato, San Vitale, Bolognina, Navile and Massarenti are usually cheaper with good buses; Saragozza, Murri and the hills (*Colli*) are quieter.
- **Avoid scams:** never pay a deposit before seeing the room (in person or by live video) and the contract; avoid cash without a receipt; be suspicious of prices far below the market; talk to current flatmates before signing.

## 6. Getting around

- **TPER** runs the buses. A single urban ticket costs **€2.30** and is valid **75 minutes** after validation; on board it costs **€2.50**. The 10-ride **City Pass** costs **€1.90 a ride** [S12]. You can also pay by tapping a contactless card on board [S12].
- **UniBo annual bus pass:** **€161** for master's students **under 27**, **€187** for those **27 or older** [S10]. Buy it in **Studenti Online** until **15 December 2026**, while funds last, and collect it at the TPER ticket office, **Via Marconi 4** [S10].
- **Extra €100 discount:** UniBo runs a yearly call for students with an ISEE (Italian income indicator) up to €35,000. For 2026/27 the deadline was 24 September 2026 [S10]. Next cohorts: watch for it in September.
- **Airport (Bologna Marconi):** the **Marconi Express** to Bologna Centrale takes about 7 minutes and costs **€12.80** one way [S13].
- **Trains:** Bologna Centrale is a major rail hub (Trenitalia and Italo). UniBo students get the Trenitalia **Welfare PLUS** offer and discounted **Itabus** coach fares [S11].
- **Tram:** a tram line (the Red Line) is being built. Opening date: **not verified**. Check the [Comune di Bologna](https://www.comune.bologna.it/) news.
- **Bikes and walking:** the centre is compact and walkable. The historic centre is a limited-traffic zone (**ZTL**); don't drive in without a permit.
- **Apps:** Roger (TPER tickets), Google Maps and Moovit for routes.

## 7. Money and phone

- **Bank:** many students start with Revolut, Wise or N26 and open an Italian account later only if needed (some landlords prefer an Italian IBAN). Opening a bank account requires the tax code [S6].
- **Payments:** cards are widely accepted; **Satispay** is a popular Italian payment app. Keep some cash for small bars and markets.
- **Phone:** low-cost operators include Iliad, ho. Mobile, Very Mobile and Kena; big operators are TIM, Vodafone and WindTre. What you need to buy a SIM (passport, tax code) and current prices: **not verified**. Ask in the shop. A travel eSIM can cover the first days.

## 9. Study places and campus

- **Where EU-HEM teaching happens:** at the Department of Economics in Bologna [S1]; your timetable on the course website shows the rooms.
- **Study rooms** [S14]:
  - **La Scuderia (ER.GO):** Piazza Verdi 2, every day 9:00–21:00
  - **Palazzo Hercolani:** Strada Maggiore 45, Mon–Fri 9:00–19:00
  - **ER.GO Residence Carducci** (Via Gandusio 14) and **Umberto Eco** (Via San Petronio Vecchio 28–30): every day 9:00–24:00
- **Affluences app:** shows in real time how many seats are free [S14].
- **SPEIS:** UniBo's booking service for study and group rooms [S14].
- **Libraries:** the University Library of Bologna (BUB), and the public libraries **Salaborsa** (Piazza Nettuno) and **Archiginnasio**. Opening hours change; check before you go.

## 10. Food and daily life

- **University canteens (ER.GO):** open to UniBo students, with lower prices when you show your badge. Students who apply to ER.GO's call (published in July) can get prepaid meals [S15].
- **Eat your own food:** refreshment points with microwaves at Via Petroni (corner of Piazza Verdi, Mon–Fri 12:00–15:00) and at the Centro Polifunzionale Unione (Mon–Fri 11:30–15:30) [S15].
- **Supermarkets:** Coop, Conad, Esselunga, Lidl, Eurospin, Pam, Carrefour. Many small shops close on Sundays or have shorter hours.
- **Markets:** Mercato delle Erbe (Via Ugo Bassi), Mercato di Mezzo (Quadrilatero, near Piazza Maggiore), Mercato Albani (Bolognina).

## 11. Weather and what to pack

- Average temperature: about **3.5 °C in January** (0 to 7 °C) and **25.5 °C in July** (19 to 31 °C). About 670 mm of rain a year, with more in spring and autumn (1991–2020 averages, based on ARPAE data) [S18].
- **Pack:** a warm coat, scarf and gloves for winter (it can be foggy and damp), an umbrella, and light clothes for a hot summer. The porticoes keep you dry on most walks.

## 12. Sport, social life and student organisations

- **CUSB** (the university sports centre): the membership card gives insurance cover and access to sports courses and the **Alma Gym**; it is **free for first-year students**. Sign up online or at Via San Giacomo 9/2 [S16].
- **ESN Bologna:** events, trips, language tandems and the ESNcard for Erasmus and other international students; office at Via delle Belle Arti 50 [S17].
- **Your UniBo badge** gives discounts in many places; ask "*sconto studenti?*".
- **Outdoors:** Giardini Margherita, Parco della Montagnola, and the long portico walk up to the Sanctuary of San Luca.
- **Student areas:** Piazza Verdi, Via Petroni, Via del Pratello. Museums: check free-entry days and student prices.
