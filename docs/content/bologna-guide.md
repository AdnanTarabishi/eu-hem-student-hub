# Bologna — City Guide for EU-HEM Students

> Last checked: 1 October 2026. Prices and rules change often. When in doubt, the official link wins.
> Unofficial student-run guide — not affiliated with EU-HEM or the University of Bologna.

---

## 1. First-week checklist

1. **Codice fiscale (Italian tax code)** — you need it for almost everything: university enrolment, rent contract, SIM card, bank, healthcare.
2. **Residence permit (non-EU students only)** — apply within **8 working days** of arriving in Italy.
3. **Health coverage** — EHIC (EU students) or SSN registration / private insurance (non-EU).
4. **Italian SIM card** — needs your passport/ID + codice fiscale.
5. **Bus pass** — buy the discounted UniBo annual TPER pass through Studenti Online.
6. **University accounts** — Studenti Online, Virtuale, institutional email, student badge.
7. **EU citizens staying more than 3 months** — register your residence at the city registry office (Anagrafe).

---

## 2. Documents & bureaucracy

### Codice fiscale (tax code)
- A 16-character personal code used for tax and administration.
- Free. Non-EU students often receive it from the Italian embassy/consulate together with the visa. Otherwise, request it at the Agenzia delle Entrate (Italian Revenue Agency).
- A printout or official email is usually accepted; you don't need a plastic card.

### Residence permit — *permesso di soggiorno* (non-EU students)
- Required if you stay in Italy more than 90 days. Apply within **8 working days** of arrival.
- Get the application kit (yellow-striped envelope) at a post office with a **"Sportello Amico"** counter, or from the UniBo International Student Administration Office (first-year students can get help from a tutor there).
- Main post office in Bologna: **Piazza Minghetti**.
- At the post office you submit the kit and pay the fees (around **€116 in total**: permit card, revenue stamp and postal fee — check the exact amounts at the counter).
- You receive a **postal receipt** (keep it safe — it proves you applied) and an **appointment at the Questura** (police immigration office) for fingerprints.
- **Renewal:** apply at least **60 days before** your permit expires.
- Official info: https://www.unibo.it/en/study/enrolment-fees-and-other-procedures/residence-permit

## 3. Healthcare

### EU students
- Bring your **European Health Insurance Card (EHIC)**. It gives access to necessary care in the public system.

### Non-EU students — two options
| Option | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **SSN registration** (Italian National Health Service) | Minimum **€700 per calendar year** | Covers 1 Jan – 31 Dec, not reduced for shorter periods. You can pay the current and the next year together to cover a full academic year. Gives you a general practitioner (GP). |
| **Private insurance** | Varies | No GP assigned. You usually pay first and claim a refund later. Check limits carefully. |

- Official info: https://www.unibo.it/en/study/life-at-university-and-in-the-city/health-and-assistance/health-and-medical-assistance-in-bologna-cesena-forli-ravenna-e-rimini/medical-assistance-for-international-students

### Good to know
- **Emergency: call 112** (works for everything: medical, police, fire).
- **Free basic medical care for non-resident students** has been offered through a UniBo / CUSB agreement — check the UniBo portal for the current service and how to book.
- **Psychological support:** UniBo offers a free psychological support service for students.
- Pharmacies (*farmacia*, green cross sign) can advise on minor problems; a rotating night/holiday pharmacy is always open.

## 4. Housing

### What it costs (2026)
| Type | Typical monthly price |
|---|---|
| Single room in a shared flat | **€450–650** (average around €600 in March 2026) |
| Bed in a shared double room | €300–360 |
| Studio | €800–1,000 |
| One-bedroom flat | around €1,200 |
| Utilities (per person, shared flat) | €70–150 |

Bologna's rents have risen sharply in recent years — start looking early.

### Where to look
- **ER.GO residences** (the regional student services agency): subsidised rooms, assigned through a yearly call based on merit and income.
- Websites/apps: Idealista, Immobiliare.it, Coinquilino, HousingAnywhere, Uniplaces, Spotahome.
- Student Facebook/Telegram groups and word of mouth (ask older EU-HEM students!).

### Neighbourhoods
- **Zona Universitaria / centre** — walkable, lively, most expensive, can be noisy at night.
- **San Donato / San Vitale, Bolognina, Navile, Massarenti** — usually cheaper, good bus connections.
- **Saragozza / Murri / Colli** — quieter and residential.

### Avoid scams
- Never pay a deposit before seeing the room (in person or by live video call) and seeing the contract.
- Make sure the contract is **registered** — it protects you and you may need it for the residence permit.
- Avoid paying in cash without a receipt.
- If the price is far below market, be suspicious.
- Talk to current flatmates before signing.

## 5. Getting around

### Buses (TPER)
- Single urban ticket: **€2.30**, valid **75 minutes** (prices in effect since March 2025).
- Pay by **tapping your contactless card or phone** on board (same €2.30), or buy at tobacconists/newsagents. Paying with coins on board costs more.
- 10-ride City Pass available.
- Night buses run Friday–Sunday and on holidays.
- App: **Roger** (TPER's official app) for tickets; Google Maps / Moovit for routes.

### UniBo discounted annual bus pass ⭐
- Annual TPER pass for the Bologna area at a subsidised price: **€161** for second-cycle (master's) students.
- Buy it through **Studenti Online** — available until **15 December 2026** while funds last.
- Collect it at the **TPER ticket office, Via Marconi 4**.
- Each year UniBo also runs a call for an **extra €100 discount** for a limited number of students — for 2026/27 the deadline was 24 September 2026. **Next cohort: watch for it in September!**
- Official info: https://www.unibo.it/en/study/life-at-university-and-in-the-city/transport-and-mobility/transport-and-mobility-subsidies-for-students

### Airport (Bologna Marconi, BLQ)
- **Marconi Express** monorail to Bologna Centrale: about 7 minutes, **€12.80** (includes 75 minutes on TPER city buses).
- Cheapest option: **TPER bus line Q**, €2.30.

### Trains
- **Bologna Centrale** is a major rail hub — fast trains to Milan, Florence, Rome, Venice.
- Operators: Trenitalia and Italo.
- UniBo students get discounts on Trenitalia "Freccia" trains and on Itabus coaches — see the UniBo transport page.

### Bikes & walking
- The centre is compact and very walkable — and famous for its **porticoes** (covered walkways, UNESCO World Heritage).
- Bike sharing apps are available; UniBo has a bike project for students (AlmaBike).
- A new **tram line (Red Line)** is being built and is expected to open in 2026.
- Cars: the historic centre is a limited traffic zone (**ZTL**) — don't drive in without a permit.

## 6. Phone & internet

- To buy any Italian SIM you need your **passport (or EU ID) + codice fiscale**.
- Low-cost operators popular with students: **Iliad** (self-service "Simbox" kiosks in malls), **ho. Mobile**, **Very Mobile**, **Kena**. Big operators: TIM, Vodafone, WindTre.
- Typical student plans: about **€5–10 per month** with lots of data.
- Tip: use a travel eSIM for your first days until you have your codice fiscale.

---

## 7. Money & banking

- Many students start with **Revolut, Wise or N26** and open an Italian account later only if needed (some landlords prefer an Italian IBAN).
- **Satispay** is a popular Italian payment app; cards are widely accepted, but keep some cash for small bars and markets.

---

## 8. Food & groceries

- Supermarkets: Coop, Conad, Esselunga, Lidl, Eurospin, Pam, Carrefour. Many smaller shops close on Sunday or have reduced hours.
- Markets: **Mercato delle Erbe** (Via Ugo Bassi), **Mercato di Mezzo** (Quadrilatero, near Piazza Maggiore), **Mercato Albani** (Bolognina).
- **ER.GO university canteens:** open to UniBo students; students who apply to the ER.GO call can get prepaid meal credits. There are also "refreshment points" where you can eat food from home.
- Save money: Too Good To Go app, cooking with flatmates, and the Italian *aperitivo*.

## 9. Study places

- **UniBo study rooms**, for example:
  - **La Scuderia (ER.GO)** — Piazza Verdi 2, open daily 9:00–21:00
  - **Palazzo Hercolani** — Strada Maggiore 45, Mon–Fri 9:00–19:00
  - **ER.GO Residence Carducci** (Via Gandusio 14) and **Umberto Eco** (Via San Petronio Vecchio 28–30), daily 9:00–24:00
- **Affluences app:** check in real time how many seats are free in UniBo libraries and study rooms.
- **SPEIS:** UniBo booking service for study and group-work rooms.
- **Biblioteca Universitaria di Bologna (BUB):** the University's central historic library.
- Public libraries: **Biblioteca Salaborsa** (Piazza Nettuno) and **Archiginnasio**.
- Opening hours change — check before you go.
- Official list: https://www.unibo.it/en/study/life-at-university-and-in-the-city/libraries-digital-resources-and-study-rooms/study-rooms-in-bologna

## 10. Sport & outdoors

- **CUSB** — the University Sports Centre: courses, gyms and teams for UniBo students.
- Running and parks: **Giardini Margherita**, **Parco della Montagnola**, and the long portico walk up to the **Sanctuary of San Luca** (great workout and view).

---

## 11. Social life, culture & discounts

- Your **UniBo badge** gives discounts in many places — always ask "*sconto studenti?*".
- Erasmus/international student associations (e.g. **ESN Bologna**) organise trips and events open to international students.
- Museums: check free-entry days and student prices.
- Classic student areas: Piazza Verdi, Via Petroni, Via del Pratello.
