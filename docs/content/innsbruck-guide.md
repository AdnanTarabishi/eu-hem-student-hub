EU-HEM at Innsbruck is hosted by **MCI | The Entrepreneurial School**. The joint master's programme is full-time and taught in English [S1]. MCI's main address is **Universitätsstraße 15, 6020 Innsbruck** [S2].

The first practical priority is **housing**. MCI says student residences fill quickly and recommends beginning the search as soon as you are admitted [S4]. For 2026, WIST publishes some of the clearest student-housing prices in Innsbruck, with single rooms starting roughly between **€278 and €445 per month** depending on the residence and room type [S9].

The second priority is Austrian registration. **Everyone who moves into accommodation in Innsbruck must register the address within 3 days**. You need a completed *Meldezettel* signed by your accommodation provider [S15].

For non-EU students, do **not** automatically assume that you need a new Austrian residence permit. Austria has a special mobility route allowing eligible students with a valid **"Student" residence title from another EU member state** and a qualifying mobility programme/agreement to stay in Austria for up to **360 days** [S17]. This rule says *EU member state*, not simply EEA or Schengen state: for example, Norway is in Schengen/EEA but is not an EU member state, so a Norwegian permit by itself should not be assumed to qualify [S17][S35]. Ask MCI and/or OeAD to confirm your individual immigration route before relying on it.

## 2. Before you move

Your exact Innsbruck semester dates and teaching schedule should come from EU-HEM/MCI. MCI publishes your preliminary timetable in myMCI [S3].

### As soon as your Innsbruck semester is confirmed

- **Housing:** start immediately. MCI says demand is high and student residences fill quickly [S4].
- **Check your immigration route if you are non-EU.** If you already hold a qualifying study residence title from another EU member state and EU-HEM/MCI confirms that the mobility-programme conditions are met, the special 360-day mobility route may apply [S17].
- **If you are arriving from Norway or another non-EU Schengen/EEA state:** do not assume that country's permit is enough for the Austrian 360-day student-mobility rule. Confirm your case with MCI/OeAD [S17][S35].
- **Insurance:** EU/EEA students should arrange an EHIC or suitable insurance; non-EU students should verify whether their immigration route requires travel insurance or Austrian-style "all risks" insurance [S17][S21][S23].
- **Check the age rules for transport.** The discounted Innsbruck semester pass is only for students **under 27** [S24].

### About 1–2 months before arrival

- Ask your landlord or dormitory whether they will sign the **Meldezettel**; you need the accommodation provider's signature for residence registration [S15].
- If you need an ordinary Austrian student residence permit, start early. MCI warns that visa procedures may take several months [S1].
- If your stay is **91 days to 6 months** and the special mobility route does not apply, an Austrian **Visa D** can be the relevant short-stay student route; for a stay over 6 months, the **Residence Permit – Student** route is generally relevant [S18][S19].
- Download **VVT Smartride** for local route planning and the **ÖBB app** for rail travel [S25][S26].
- If you will use an Austrian SIM, remember that prepaid and postpaid SIMs require identity registration [S29].

### Your first days in Innsbruck

- Register your residence with the city within **3 days** of moving in [S15].
- EU/EEA/Swiss students staying more than 3 months should also arrange the separate **registration certificate (*Anmeldebescheinigung*) within 4 months** [S15][S16].
- Activate your MCI student account when MCI sends the credentials. MCI says this normally gives access to **myMCI, Sakai and Webmail**, with the preliminary timetable in myMCI [S3].
- Attend the MCI orientation activities relevant to your intake; recent orientation programmes included the library, ÖH student union and ESN [S7].
- Save Austria's emergency numbers, especially **144** for ambulance and **140** for mountain rescue [S30][S31].

## 3. Residence and registration

Two processes are easy to confuse:

1. **Residence registration (*Meldezettel*)** — required for almost everyone moving into accommodation in Innsbruck, normally within 3 days [S15].
2. **Immigration/right-of-residence documentation** — depends on your nationality and existing residence status [S16][S17][S18][S19].

### EU/EEA students

- EU/EEA and Swiss citizens can study in Austria without a student visa.
- You still have to register your Innsbruck address. The City of Innsbruck requires residence registration within **3 days** of moving in [S15].
- The *Meldezettel* must be completed and signed by the accommodation provider. Bring the required original identity/travel documents [S15].
- Registration is **free of charge** [S15].
- In Innsbruck, the registration service is at **Maria-Theresien-Straße 18, 6020 Innsbruck** [S15].
- If you stay in Austria for **more than 3 months**, you need a separate **registration certificate (*Anmeldebescheinigung*)**, applied for within **4 months of arrival**. As a student, you generally need to show sufficient means and comprehensive health-insurance coverage [S16].
- Do not confuse the *Anmeldebescheinigung* with the *Meldezettel*. They are separate obligations.

### Non-EU students

There are several possible routes. For an EU-HEM student, **check Route A first**.

#### Route A — mobility using another EU country's "Student" residence title

Austria allows an eligible third-country student to enter and stay for up to **360 days** without obtaining a new Austrian residence title when all relevant requirements are met [S17]. These include:

- a valid residence title **"Student" from another EU member country** (titles from Ireland and Denmark do not count) for the whole Austrian stay;
- participation in a Union or multilateral mobility programme, or an agreement between two or more higher-education institutions;
- a valid travel document; and
- suitable health-insurance coverage [S17].

The OeAD page requires travel health insurance valid in Austria with cover **well over €30,000**, including possible recovery and repatriation costs, for the whole stay unless you take Austrian statutory insurance [S17].

**Important for EU-HEM:** the programme's multi-university structure makes this route particularly relevant, but the residence title itself must qualify. Do not treat any Schengen residence card as equivalent to a qualifying EU "Student" residence title.

**Norway caveat:** Norway is in the Schengen/EEA framework but is not an EU member state [S35]. Because the Austrian rule explicitly requires a "Student" residence title from another **EU member country**, a Norwegian permit should not be assumed to meet Route A. Ask MCI/OeAD for case-specific confirmation [S17].

Even under Route A, you still need the Innsbruck *Meldezettel* after moving in [S15][S17].

#### Route B — Visa C / Visa D for a stay up to 6 months

If Route A does not apply and your planned Austrian stay is no more than 6 months, OeAD says students can apply for:

- **Visa C** for up to 90 days; or
- **Visa D** for **91 days up to a maximum of 6 months** [S18].

The exact route depends on nationality, duration and your existing status. Follow MCI/OeAD instructions rather than assuming that one semester automatically means Visa D.

#### Route C — Residence Permit – Student

For a longer stay, or where your case requires it, the Austrian **Residence Permit – Student** may apply [S19].

For 2026, OeAD lists the usual proof-of-funds thresholds as [S19]:

| Applicant | Required monthly means |
|---|---:|
| Student under 24 | **€722.58/month** [S19] |
| Student age 24 or older | **€1,308.39/month** [S19] |
| Couple together | **€2,064.12/month** [S19] |
| Each child additionally | **€201.88/month** [S19] |

For the ordinary non-mobility student permit, OeAD says these funds are normally proven for up to 12 months in advance. If accommodation costs exceed **€386.43 per month**, you must show additional funds for the excess, as well as sufficient funds for health insurance [S19].

OeAD lists an application fee of **€218**, paid when you apply and not refunded if the application is refused [S19].

Residence rules are case-specific and can change. If MCI's programme-specific instructions conflict with this summary, follow MCI/OeAD and the Austrian authorities.

## 4. Healthcare

### EU/EEA students

- If you remain insured in your home country's public system, bring a valid **European Health Insurance Card (EHIC)**. OeAD says it gives access to medically necessary public healthcare in Austria under the applicable conditions [S21].
- EHIC is not comprehensive travel insurance. OeAD notes that routine check-ups are not necessarily covered and recommends additional travel insurance, especially for repatriation [S21].
- If you stay more than 3 months and apply for an *Anmeldebescheinigung*, you need comprehensive health-insurance coverage as part of the residence-right documentation [S16].

### Non-EU students

The insurance requirement depends on your immigration route.

- For the 360-day EU student-mobility route, OeAD requires travel health insurance valid in Austria with cover **well over €30,000**, including recovery and repatriation, unless you take Austrian statutory insurance [S17].
- For an Austrian **Residence Permit – Student**, travel insurance alone is not enough for the full residence-title requirement. Austria requires health insurance **covering all risks** and payable in Austria [S23].
- Eligible students can take the preferential student self-insurance of the Austrian Health Insurance Fund (**ÖGK**) after arrival. OeAD gives the premium as **€78.84 per month in 2026** [S19].

### For all students

- Austria's health advice number is **1450**. It provides telephone guidance on health problems and where to seek care [S30].
- For a medical emergency call **144** [S30].
- MCI offers student-support services such as preventive workshops, accompanying seminars and counselling; check the Student Support page for how to access them [S6].

## 5. Housing

### Start as early as possible

MCI explicitly says to secure accommodation early, ideally from the moment you are admitted, because Innsbruck's student residences fill quickly [S4].

When checked on 5 October 2026, OeAD's Innsbruck housing page showed its listed winter-semester 2026 options as fully booked, illustrating how quickly the supply can disappear [S11].

### WIST student residences

WIST is one of the major student-residence providers in Innsbruck. Its prices valid from **1 September 2026** give a useful benchmark [S9].

Examples of standard prices for a 12-month contract (1 September–31 August) include [S9]:

| Housing type | Example 2026 price |
|---|---:|
| Double-room bed | from **€218–€298/month per person** [S9] |
| Single room, sometimes with shared bathroom | from **€278–€445/month** [S9] |
| Studio / *Garçonnière* | roughly **€458–€491/month** [S9] |
| Campus Sieglanger single room | from **€388/month** [S9] |

For contracts shorter than 12 months, WIST adds a monthly surcharge of **€30–€50** to the standard price, so a one-semester stay costs more than the table shows [S9].

One especially convenient option for MCI students is **Dr. Karl Kunst Residence**, which WIST describes as being immediately next to the SOWI/MCI area. Its 2026 rates include single rooms from **€358 with shared bathroom** and **€408 with private bathroom**, with higher categories available [S10].

### OeAD housing: useful, but check EU-HEM eligibility first

MCI's current housing page describes accommodation arranged through OeAD as **"for exchange students only"** [S4]. Because EU-HEM is a joint master's programme rather than a standard exchange semester, **do not assume you are eligible**. Ask MCI International Relations before relying on OeAD's allocation service.

OeAD's current Innsbruck listings show prices roughly from **€314 to €649 per month**, depending on room type and residence [S11]. Some properties add application, booking or cleaning fees and deposits, so compare the total cost rather than only the headline rent [S12].

### Private rooms and flatshares

MCI directs students to the **ÖH Innsbruck housing marketplace** for private flats, studios and shared rooms [S4][S13]. The ÖH housing department also offers advice on student residences, flat-hunting and landlord disputes [S14].

Which neighbourhoods suit students: **not verified**. The ÖH housing team gives personal advice on residences, flatshares and rentals [S14]. Check the route to your MCI teaching building in VVT Smartride before you sign [S25].

### Scam precautions

MCI warns students **not to pay before viewing a private apartment in person** and to be suspicious of pressure for large advance payments, incomplete addresses and unusual payment requests [S4].

Whatever you rent, confirm that the accommodation provider will sign your *Meldezettel*, because you need that signature for registration [S15].

## 6. Getting around

Innsbruck is compact enough that many daily trips can be done on foot or by bike, while IVB/VVT run the local tram and bus network.

### Semester and city passes

From autumn 2026, the **Innsbruck Semester Ticket** costs **€176.70 for 6 months**. It is only for students **under 27** and gives unlimited travel on Innsbruck lines for either September–February or March–August. You must show enrolment at an Austrian higher-education institution and official photo ID [S24].

If you are 27 or older, the student semester ticket is not available. Current alternatives include [S24]:

- **Monthly Ticket Innsbruck:** **€71.80** [S24]
- **KlimaTicket Innsbruck:** **€475.20 for 1 year** [S24]
- **Single Ticket Innsbruck:** **€3.30** [S24]

For students who want transport across all Tyrol, the Semester Ticket Tirol costs **€229.90 from autumn 2026** and is also limited to students under 27 [S24].

### Apps

- **VVT Smartride:** real-time trip planning for bus, tram, rail, bike and walking connections in Tyrol and beyond [S25].
- **VVT Tickets:** local digital ticket purchase [S25].
- **ÖBB:** Austrian and international train tickets and timetable information [S26].

### Bicycle and city bike

Innsbruck has a public bike system. Since July 2026, the annual Stadtrad fee for anyone **under 26** is **€24.90**, or **€18.90 with a valid public-transport ticket** [S27].

IVB specifically says that **MCI students continue to have their own Stadtrad cooperation**, so check MCI/IVB for the current MCI-specific terms before purchasing the general tariff [S27].

### Airport and railway station

MCI says **Bus F** connects Innsbruck Airport with the city in roughly **10–15 minutes**, while Innsbruck Hauptbahnhof is close to the centre [S3].

For longer trips, MCI points to ÖBB, Nightjet, Flixbus and Trainline. Munich and Vienna are the big international airports near Innsbruck [S3].

## 7. Money and phone

### Money and banking

Austria uses the euro. If your existing account is a euro/SEPA account and works for rent, subscriptions and card payments, check whether you actually need a new Austrian account before opening one.

Whether you need an Austrian bank account for a one-semester stay: **not verified**. MCI's checklist only reminds you to close an Austrian account "if you have one" before leaving [S3].

### Phone

If your EU/EEA SIM has suitable roaming/fair-use conditions, it may be sufficient initially.

If you buy an Austrian SIM, both prepaid and postpaid connections require identity registration. Austria's telecom regulator says anonymous activation is not permitted; identification can be completed through methods such as presenting ID in a shop or supported online identification [S29].

## 8. Cost of living

MCI currently gives a rough total estimate of approximately **€1,250 per month** for a student in Innsbruck [S3]. Its individual estimates are broad and lifestyle-dependent:

| Item | MCI rough estimate |
|---|---:|
| Housing incl. utilities, single room/studio | **€400–€700/month** [S3] |
| Food and drinks | **€400–€500/month** [S3] |
| Public transport | **€35/month** [S3] |
| Cell phone | **€15/month** [S3] |
| Internet if not included in rent | **€20–€50/month** [S3] |
| Miscellaneous: books, leisure, trips, personal expenses | **€400/month** [S3] |
| **Overall** | **about €1,250/month** [S3] |

The line items are not meant to be mechanically added at their maximum values; MCI explicitly describes them as rough estimates [S3].

For comparison, Austria's official Study in Austria portal gives a nationwide average student cost of about **€1,300 per month** [S28].

A WIST dorm can reduce housing costs considerably compared with a private studio, while skiing, frequent eating out and regional travel can push the budget upward.

## 10. Food and daily life

### Groceries

Austria has many discount supermarkets with reasonably priced food. Study in Austria gives a student dinner at about €12 and a coffee at about €3.50 as typical prices [S28]. Cooking regularly keeps the budget down.

### Near the university/MCI area

The University of Innsbruck's **SOWI Lounge** opened in 2025 and provides coffee, snacks, drinks and several cooked lunch options on weekdays. It is in the SOWI area close to MCI's central location [S33].

### Practical habits

- Carry a reusable water bottle; Innsbruck is an easy city for walking and outdoor activity.
- Learn a few basic German phrases even though EU-HEM is in English. English works well in academic settings, but German is useful with landlords, smaller businesses and administration.
- Shop opening hours on Sundays and public holidays: **not verified**. Check before you rely on a shop being open.

## 11. Weather and what to pack

Innsbruck sits in an Alpine valley, so seasons are more pronounced than in Bologna or Rotterdam.

Climate normals for Innsbruck Airport (1991–2020, based on GeoSphere Austria data) show approximately [S32]:

| Month | Average | Average low | Average high |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| January | about **−1 °C** | about **−5 °C** | about **4 °C** [S32] |
| July | about **19 °C** | about **14 °C** | about **26 °C** [S32] |

Snow and freezing nights are normal in winter, while summer days can be warm. Mountain weather can change much more quickly than conditions in the city valley.

### What to pack

For an autumn/winter semester:
- warm waterproof coat;
- layers, hat and gloves;
- waterproof shoes with good grip;
- compact rain jacket;
- sunglasses and sunscreen—the mountains amplify sun exposure;
- suitable footwear if you plan to hike;
- a small daypack and water bottle.

For mountain activities, city clothing is not enough. Check conditions and use appropriate footwear/equipment; save **140** for mountain rescue [S31].

## 12. Sport, social life and student organisations

Innsbruck's strongest advantage is access to outdoor sport. MCI highlights skiing, snowboarding, tobogganing, hiking, mountain biking and climbing as major local activities [S4].

### University sport

MCI has a cooperation with the **University Sports Institute Innsbruck (USI)**. MCI says students can access a very large programme of sports courses and facilities at student-friendly conditions [S4].

### Student organisations and orientation

Recent MCI orientation programmes explicitly introduced students to:

- the **Austrian Student Union (ÖH)**;
- **Erasmus Student Network (ESN)**;
- MCI Library Services; and
- social/city activities [S7].

MCI also operates a **buddy system** for incoming international/exchange students. Buddies contact students before arrival and help with networking, daily life and administrative procedures [S8]. Because the current buddy description is written for exchange students, ask MCI whether EU-HEM students are included in the current intake's buddy programme.

### Outdoor safety

Being close to the mountains makes spontaneous hiking and skiing easy, but Alpine conditions should be treated seriously. For a mountain emergency call **140** [S31]. Check weather, route difficulty and avalanche information when relevant before going into the mountains.
