EU-HEM teaching at Erasmus University Rotterdam is hosted by the **Erasmus School of Health Policy & Management (ESHPM)** at **Campus Woudestein**, Burgemeester Oudlaan 50. The programme is taught in English [S1][S30].

The biggest practical issue is **housing**. EUR says Rotterdam has a severe housing shortage, students are responsible for finding their own accommodation, and the university's reserved supply covers only about **25% of incoming international students** [S3][S4].

For students moving to Rotterdam for **spring 2027**, there is a time-sensitive housing date: SSH registration for the spring semester opens **12 October 2026 at 12:00 CEST**, and approved students can start booking **19 October 2026 at 12:00 CEST** [S5]. However, the currently advertised spring supply is specifically labelled for **spring exchange students**, and both reserved options require you to be **30 or younger** and starting your first year at EUR. EU-HEM is a joint master's programme, so **confirm with ESHPM/EU-HEM that you are eligible before relying on those rooms** [S5][S6][S42].

**Non-EU students already holding a study residence permit from another EU country should check the intra-EU student mobility route before starting a new Dutch permit application.** Under Directive (EU) 2016/801, eligible students in a multi-country programme can study in the Netherlands for up to 360 days using the first country's study permit after the required notification; this is not automatic, so ask EUR Team Immigration to confirm your case [S16].

## 2. Before you move

Your exact Rotterdam dates depend on the EU-HEM mobility plan sent to you. Use EU-HEM/ESHPM instructions for the academic calendar and any programme-specific arrival tasks [S2][S42].

### About 3–4 months before arrival

- **Start housing immediately.** EUR advises beginning at least **3 months before arrival** and securing accommodation before travelling [S4].
- **Check EUR reserved housing, but do not rely on it.** Only about 25% of incoming international students can be covered by reserved rooms [S3][S4].
- **Ask how EU-HEM students are classified for spring housing.** The current SSH and The Social Hub spring pages use “spring exchange student” eligibility language [S5][S6].
- **Non-EU students:** contact EUR Team Immigration and first ask whether **intra-EU mobility under Directive (EU) 2016/801** applies to your existing study permit. Do not assume that any Schengen or Italian residence permit is sufficient [S15][S16].
- **Insurance:** arrange adequate health coverage for the Netherlands. Students who are in the Netherlands only to study are **not legally allowed** to take Dutch basic health insurance; this changes if you work or do a paid internship [S20].
- **Documents:** keep passport/ID, admission or enrolment proof, housing contract, insurance documents and immigration correspondence available in digital and paper copies.

### About 1–2 months before arrival

- **Check whether your housing permits municipal registration.** This matters for the BSN and many administrative tasks. If you will stay more than 4 months, your address normally needs to be registrable [S8][S11].
- **Plan municipal registration.** If you will live in Rotterdam for more than 4 months and have never been registered in the Netherlands, EUR says you can start the online first-registration process **4 weeks before arrival** [S11].
- **If using the ordinary non-EU Dutch residence route:** follow the deadline shown in your EUR immigration case. EUR, as recognised sponsor, handles the study-permit procedure with the IND [S15][S17].
- **Plan the trip from the airport.** Schiphol Airport has frequent direct Intercity trains to Rotterdam and the journey is about 40 minutes [S22].
- **Do not buy a long public-transport subscription before you know your commute.** You can initially check in and out using a debit/credit card or phone through OVpay [S22].
- **Phone:** for a short stay, EUR recommends prepaid SIM or eSIM; a free Lebara SIM is normally available from the ESN office on Campus Woudestein [S29].

### Your first week

- Complete your **municipality/BSN registration** if required [S11].
- Register with a **GP (huisarts)** as soon as possible; the GP is normally the first point of contact for medical and mental-health problems [S21].
- Confirm access to **MyEUR, Canvas and Osiris**, and set up your digital student card [S33].
- Learn the route between your home and Campus Woudestein using **9292** [S27].
- Decide whether to use a bicycle, OVpay, a RET discount product or—if you meet DUO conditions—the Dutch student travel product [S22][S26].
- Save **112** and the EUR campus emergency number **+31 10 408 1100** [S39].

## 3. Residence and registration

Municipal registration/BSN and immigration status are **two different processes**. You may need to do both [S11][S15].

### EU/EEA students

- EU/EEA and Swiss citizens do not need a Dutch visa or residence permit for study [S14].
- If you will stay in the Netherlands for **more than 4 months**, and you have not previously been registered, register with the municipality where you live. EUR says the Rotterdam online process can start 4 weeks before arrival [S11].
- When booking the Rotterdam appointment, EUR recommends choosing **“Studentenafspraak gemeente”**. The special student procedure is intended to provide the BSN immediately at the appointment; the regular procedure may take longer [S11].
- Bring your case number, appointment QR code, valid passport or ID and proof of your living situation such as a signed rental contract [S11].
- If your stay is **less than 4 months** but you need a BSN, you can use the non-resident register (RNI). Follow the current instructions on the EUR registration page [S11][S13].

### Non-EU students

There are two very different routes. **Check Route A first.**

#### Route A — intra-EU student mobility

You may be able to study in the Netherlands **without obtaining a new Dutch residence permit** if all the IND requirements for intra-EU student mobility are met [S16]. Key conditions include:

- you already hold a valid **study residence permit in another EU country** under Directive (EU) 2016/801;
- you are following an educational programme that involves study in several countries, or you come under an agreement between higher-education institutions;
- the Dutch host is an IND-recognised sponsor;
- the first-country study permit remains valid throughout the Dutch stay;
- the Dutch study period is no more than **360 days**; and
- you meet the financial and other IND conditions [S16].

The required notification can be submitted by the student or the Dutch institution, and the IND says the notification itself is free of charge [S16].

**For EU-HEM:** this route is highly relevant, but eligibility is case-specific. Ask EUR Team Immigration whether your existing permit qualifies under the Directive and what notification/proof they need. Do not rely on the fact that you simply hold an Italian or other Schengen residence card [S16][S42].

#### Route B — ordinary Dutch study residence procedure

If Route A does not apply, EUR determines whether you need an **MVV entry visa, a Dutch residence permit, or both** and starts the procedure as your recognised sponsor [S15][S17].

For an ordinary first immigration application, EUR currently lists an immigration fee of **€254** and asks for a refundable living-fee deposit of **€1,150 for each month** of study in the Netherlands, up to **€13,800 for 12 months** [S18]. These amounts apply to the ordinary EUR immigration workflow and should not be assumed to apply to the intra-EU mobility notification route.

Follow the deadline in your personal EUR immigration case rather than using a generic deadline [S18].

#### Municipality registration and BSN

- If you will live in the Netherlands for more than 4 months, you normally also need municipal registration even after the immigration route is settled [S11].
- The standard EUR checklist for non-EU first registration asks for your passport, proof of address and a residence permit/MVV or—where applicable—an IND approval letter [S11].
- If you are using intra-EU mobility rather than an ordinary Dutch permit, confirm with EUR and the municipality which IND proof you should bring to the registration appointment.

## 4. Healthcare

### EU/EEA students

- If you are in the Netherlands **for study only**, you are **not legally allowed** to take Dutch basic health insurance. Arrange adequate cover through your home-country policy and/or international student insurance [S20].
- You may be eligible to use an **EHIC** for necessary care during a temporary stay. EUR warns that EHIC coverage is limited and does not replace broader travel/health insurance [S20].
- If you take a (part-time) job, Dutch basic health insurance is required from your first day of work, and the EHIC is no longer valid. A **paid internship** also makes Dutch basic health insurance required [S20].

### Non-EU students

- If you are in the Netherlands **for study only**, arrange international student health insurance or adequate qualifying home-country coverage; you are **not legally allowed** to take Dutch basic health insurance [S20].
- If you take a (part-time) job, Dutch basic health insurance is required from your first day of work. For a paid internship it is required if you earn at least the Dutch minimum wage [S20].
- Check the insurance situation again before accepting a job or paid internship rather than assuming your student policy remains sufficient.

### For all students

- Register with a **GP (huisarts)** soon after arrival. The GP is the normal first point of contact and usually refers you to specialists or hospital care when needed [S21].
- For an urgent problem outside normal GP hours that is **not life-threatening**, use the regional out-of-hours GP service (Huisartsenposten Rijnmond) and **call first** [S40].
- For a life-threatening emergency, call **112** [S39].
- EUR offers university psychologists and Student Wellbeing resources for psychological, social or emotional problems that affect study [S21][S41].

## 5. Housing

### Start early

Housing is the single biggest practical risk. EUR says there is a severe shortage in Rotterdam and the Netherlands, and explicitly advises international students to **secure housing before travelling** [S3][S4].

EUR advises starting at least **3 months before arrival**. Its reserved rooms cover only about **25% of incoming international students**, so most students must also search the private market [S3][S4].

### What to budget

EUR's current estimate for furnished accommodation is around **€700–€1,000 per month**. Utilities may or may not be included, so check the contract carefully [S4][S10].

### EUR reserved housing

**SSH:** for spring 2026–27, EUR says it has around 175 rooms for spring exchange students. Registration opens **12 October 2026 at 12:00 CEST**, and booking for approved students starts **19 October 2026 at 12:00 CEST**. Short-stay prices are about **€765–€870 per month including services** [S5].

SSH uses fixed rental periods. The spring options currently shown are 30 December 2026–31 May 2027, 30 December 2026–29 July 2027, or 1 February 2027–29 July 2027 [S5].

**Important EU-HEM caveat:** the spring SSH allocation is currently described as being for “spring exchange students”. SSH's criteria also include being a non-Dutch national starting your first year at EUR and being **no older than 30** at the start of the academic year. Confirm your eligibility with ESHPM/EU-HEM before relying on it [S5][S42].

**The Social Hub:** EUR currently lists 37 reserved rooms for spring exchange students, with booking opening **21 October 2026 at 12:00 CEST**. The listed spring rate is **€1,438.51 per month** for 4 January–30 June 2027 [S6]. The same age limit (**not older than 30**) and first-year criteria apply, and the page says bookings by students who do not meet the eligibility will be cancelled, so treat this as an option only after EUR confirms your status [S6].

### Private market and areas to search

- Do not search only in the most popular areas. EUR says most students prefer **Kralingen, City Centre, Delfshaven and Noord** [S7].
- Widen the search to nearby places such as **Capelle aan den IJssel and Schiedam**. EUR notes that commuting for students is normal and many journeys within Rotterdam are manageable in roughly 30 minutes by public transport or bike [S7].
- Use EUR's official **Private Market** page and housing selection guide as your starting point for platforms and agencies rather than relying on random social-media advertisements [S9].
- For stays over 4 months, make sure the landlord allows you to **register at the address** [S8][S11].
- Always insist on a written rental contract. Check rent, utilities, municipal taxes, deposit, registration permission, start/end dates and notice rules before paying [S8].

### Scam warning

Treat these as red flags: a price far below the market, pressure to pay immediately, refusal to provide a contract or viewing, payment requested before the agreement is clear, or a landlord who cannot demonstrate the right to rent the property [S8]. EUR advises contacting its Student Housing team if you have doubts [S3][S4].

## 6. Getting around

Rotterdam is very bike- and public-transport friendly. For most EU-HEM students, the best strategy is to start with **OVpay plus cycling**, then buy a discount product only after you know your actual commute [S22].

### Bicycle

EUR strongly recommends cycling and estimates a reasonable second-hand bicycle at around **€150** [S22]. Buy from a legitimate shop or seller; EUR specifically warns against suspiciously cheap street bikes because they may be stolen [S22].

Campus Woudestein is about a **15-minute bike ride from the city centre** [S30].

### Public transport in Rotterdam

- **RET** runs local metro, tram and bus services. **NS** is the national rail operator [S22].
- With **OVpay**, you can check in and out using a debit card, credit card or compatible phone. Always use the same card/device to check in and out [S22].
- Use **9292** for door-to-door planning across operators [S27].
- RET's current monthly discount products cost **€8.20 for 20% off** RET fares or **€29.70 for 40% off** RET fares [S25][S44]. These are discount subscriptions, not unlimited passes. They are valid on RET buses, trams and metro, but not on neighbourhood buses, night buses or other transport companies [S25][S44].
- A Dutch **student travel product** can provide free or discounted national public transport, but foreign students do **not** receive it automatically. Eligibility follows DUO student-finance conditions (nationality, full-time study), and you must be **under 30** when your student finance starts [S26].

### Getting to Campus Woudestein

From Rotterdam Centraal, current EUR directions include tram connections toward Woudestein/De Esch and metro connections via the city centre to **Kralingse Zoom**, followed by about a 10-minute walk. Check 9292 on the day because lines can change [S23][S27].

### Airports and trains

- **Amsterdam Schiphol:** direct Intercity trains run frequently; EUR estimates about **40 minutes** to Rotterdam [S22].
- **Rotterdam The Hague Airport:** reachable by bus and metro [S22].
- For trips to Amsterdam, The Hague, Utrecht and other Dutch cities, use NS and compare off-peak products only if you will travel frequently.

## 7. Money and phone

### Bank account

A Dutch bank account is useful but not always the first thing you need. EUR says it can be required or helpful for monthly tuition payments, some phone contracts and refunding an immigration living-fee deposit [S28].

A **BSN** is required by many banks. EUR notes that some providers let international students open an account before receiving the BSN and submit it later; check its current bank-account comparison because bank conditions change [S28].

If your existing European/SEPA account and card work for your needs, you may not need to rush into opening another account.

### Payments

Card/contactless payment is widespread. Public transport can be paid directly with a debit/credit card or compatible phone through OVpay [S22].

### Phone

For a short stay, EUR recommends a **prepaid SIM or eSIM**. Its 2026–27 arrival factsheet says students can normally pick up a free Lebara SIM at the ESN office on Campus Woudestein [S29].

If you keep an EU/EEA SIM, check your provider's fair-use/roaming rules for a multi-month stay before deciding whether you need a Dutch number.

## 8. Cost of living

EUR's current overall estimate is **€1,000–€1,800 per month**, excluding tuition fees and unexpected costs [S10]. Housing is the biggest reason the range is wide.

EUR's indicative monthly figures are [S10]:

| Item | Approximate amount |
|---|---|
| Furnished accommodation including shared or individual utilities | **€700–€1,000** [S10] |
| Insurance | **€56** [S10] |
| Books and study materials | **€50–€150** [S10] |
| Daily expenses: food, drinks, clothing, social life, public transport, phone and sport | **€450** [S10] |
| **Overall student budget** | **€1,000–€1,800** [S10] |

These are broad planning figures, not a bill to add mechanically; some categories overlap with personal lifestyle choices.

For an ordinary non-EU residence-permit case, do not confuse the monthly cost-of-living estimate with EUR's immigration proof-of-funds process: EUR currently requests **€1,150 per month** as a refundable living-fee deposit in that workflow [S18].

### Saving money

EUR recommends discount supermarkets such as **Lidl, Dirk and Aldi**, the **Blaak market on Tuesdays and Saturdays**, cooking at home, and food-saving services such as **Too Good To Go** [S10].

## 9. Study places and campus

EU-HEM at EUR is based at **Campus Woudestein**, Burgemeester Oudlaan 50, 3062 PA Rotterdam [S1][S30].

### Study spaces

EUR lists student study spaces in the **Polak Building, Sanders Building and University Library**. MyEUR carries the most up-to-date overview [S31].

The University Library reports **960 bookable study spaces**, plus access to databases, e-books and e-journals [S32].

### Student systems

- **MyEUR:** central student portal and current campus/study-space information.
- **Canvas:** course materials, announcements and learning activities.
- **Osiris:** registration and academic administration.
- **Digital student card:** available after enrolment; follow EUR's current instructions for the photo and activation [S33].

### Useful campus services

Campus Woudestein includes the University Library, study buildings, Erasmus Food Plaza, sports facilities and other student services [S30][S34].

## 10. Food and daily life

### Groceries and saving

For lower-cost groceries, EUR specifically suggests **Lidl, Dirk and Aldi**. The Blaak market is a useful option for fresh food, especially on **Tuesdays and Saturdays** [S10].

**Too Good To Go** can be useful for discounted surplus food [S10].

### On campus

**Erasmus Food Plaza** and other cafés/food outlets are available on Campus Woudestein [S34]. Check the current campus page before relying on opening hours, because they vary during holidays and exam periods.

### Eating out and student life

EUR's arrival guide highlights the university area and central Rotterdam for budget-friendly and international food options [S29]. Cooking with flatmates is still the simplest way to keep the monthly budget under control.

## 11. Weather and what to pack

Rotterdam has a maritime climate: generally milder than Oslo but often **windy, wet and changeable**.

KNMI's 1991–2020 climate normals for Rotterdam show [S38]:

| Month | Average | Average minimum | Average maximum |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| January | 4.0 °C | 1.3 °C | 6.4 °C |
| July | 18.2 °C | 13.4 °C | 22.7 °C |

The annual average is about 10.7 °C, with substantial rainfall through the year [S38].

### What to pack

For a spring stay, bring:
- a waterproof outer layer and shoes that cope with rain;
- layers rather than one extremely heavy coat;
- gloves and a warm hat for January/February;
- wind-resistant clothing;
- rain gear suitable for cycling; and
- bike lights/reflective items if you plan to cycle after dark.

An umbrella is useful, but strong wind makes a waterproof jacket more dependable.

## 12. Sport, social life and student organisations

### Sport

**Erasmus Sport** operates the university sports facilities. Current regular prices include a 6-month pass with gym for **€205**, or a 6-month pass without the gym package for **€135** [S35]. Check the live price page before buying because temporary promotions can change [S35].

### International and student organisations

EUR lists many academic, cultural, social and international organisations. **ESN Rotterdam** is described by EUR as its largest international student organisation and focuses on building community among local and international students [S36].

The university also runs orientation programmes for incoming international students at the beginning of semesters. Check the Orientation & Arrival page for the spring event dates once they are published [S43].

### Dutch language

The programme is taught in English, but basic Dutch is useful for daily life and work. EUR's Language & Training Centre offers Dutch courses, with course rounds starting in **September, January and April** [S37].

### Outdoors and culture

Kralingse Bos is a popular green area close to the university side of the city, while Rotterdam's museums, cinemas, music venues and event calendar give plenty of options beyond campus [S29].
