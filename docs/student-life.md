# Student Life Guide and Moving Between Cities

The Life section adds practical guidance shared across the two-year programme. The
Student Life Guide organises six everyday topics; Moving Between Cities organises
six stages from preparation to departure after the programme. Both use the public
editorial content in `content/life-guide.json` and connect students to the existing
city guides, Support & Contacts, Academic Rules, Toolkit and future careers preview.

## Content boundaries

The checklists and preparation questions are **student-made planning suggestions**.
They do not establish eligibility, diagnose a problem, calculate a legal deadline,
submit an application or replace official programme instructions. The content uses
confirmation questions for residence, employment, insurance, grants and contracts
rather than copying rules that vary by country, citizenship, enrolment and date.

Precise city facts and prices remain in `docs/content/*-guide.md`. Programme courses
remain in `content/programme.json`; host names, tracks, semester routes and thesis
choices remain in `content/tracks.json`. The frontend reads those shared helpers
when offering city and route context. Do not hard-code track journeys in Life copy:
a return to Bologna is conditional, and the final thesis destination is a choice
within the selected track's options.

Innsbruck's EU-HEM host is **MCI**, not the University of Innsbruck. An offer from a
different local institution does not establish MCI students' eligibility. MCI's
housing page explicitly labels one OeAD route exchange-only; confirm each student's
actual enrolment category before using that route.

Academic and exam adjustments link to
`academic-rules.html#special-provisions` and `support.html#contact-guide`. Keep the
existing programme workflow in its source content rather than rewriting it from a
local university service page. Local wellbeing and inclusion sources describe
practical support routes, not automatic approval of programme arrangements.

Beyond EU-HEM remains a labelled design/structure preview. Life does not turn that
preview into a published vacancy board or add job-application submission.

## JSON format

The file has `version: 1`, an editorial `reviewed` date, `topics`, `stages` and
`sources`. Text fields contain plain English text, not HTML or Markdown. Use stable,
unique kebab-case IDs and reference other objects by those IDs.

Each topic contains:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `id` | One of `housing`, `wellbeing`, `benefits`, `language`, `inclusion`, `work`. |
| `title`, `summary` | Topic heading and short introduction. |
| `icon` | Existing symbol ID in `icons.svg`. |
| `advice` | Practical suggestions; explicit official claims need a relevant reviewed source. |
| `questions` | Useful questions to ask before a decision or application. |
| `guideSections` | Existing city-guide heading slugs generated by `guideHeadingId()`. |
| `related` | Existing local Hub links, each with `label` and `href`. |
| `sourceIds` | Relevant IDs in `sources`; the source's `topics` includes the topic ID. |

Each moving stage contains `id`, `title`, `kicker`, `summary`, `tasks`,
`guideSections`, `related` and `sourceIds`. The six stage IDs are `prepare`,
`departure`, `arrival`, `settling`, `returning` and `leaving`. They describe a
general timeline, not compulsory steps for every student. Stage sources can reuse
the relevant rental, residence, service or employment resources without adding
duplicate source records.

Each source contains:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `id`, `title` | Stable reference ID and recognisable official organisation/page title. |
| `url` | Direct HTTPS link to the official resource. |
| `city` | `bologna`, `oslo`, `innsbruck`, `rotterdam` or `all` for a cross-city resource. |
| `topics` | Topic IDs that the page actually supports. |
| `checked` | Date the page was opened and its cited scope was read; otherwise `null`. |
| `status` | `verified` for that reviewed scope, or `not-verified` when it could not be checked. |
| `note` | Concise supported scope, important limits or the information still to confirm. |

`city: "all"` does not mean a rule applies identically to every destination or
student. The Your Europe sources cover EU citizens in other EU countries; their
notes explicitly direct students to separate Norwegian and non-EU guidance.

## Sources and dates

The first content review on **12 October 2026** opened 17 official sources: University
of Bologna and its Language Centre, SiO, MCI and its student portal, Erasmus
University Rotterdam, and Your Europe. These sources support specific service
routes, contract questions and eligibility cautions. We intentionally omit current
prices, detailed legal limits and application deadlines from the shared Life copy.

UiO's Norwegian-language course route could not be opened during the review. It is
stored with `status: "not-verified"` and `checked: null`. The visible note asks users
to confirm the current course page and conditions with UiO. A search result or an
older city-guide review does not count as a new verification of that page.

The file's `reviewed` date is an **editorial review date**, not a claim that every
linked service, cost, legal requirement or offer was verified that day. Source
`checked` dates describe the scope in their individual notes. The existing city
guides retain their own review dates. A presentation update must not advance either
a source date or a city-guide factual review date without opening the relevant page.

Before changing an official claim:

1. Open the official page and read the relevant passage. Prefer the host institution,
   government, municipality, public operator or recognised student welfare service.
2. Check its audience, location, student category and conditions. Keep the supported
   claim short and direct students to the full official instructions.
3. Update the source note and `checked` date only for what was actually reviewed.
   If the page is unavailable, use `not-verified` and `null` with a clear check-needed
   note; do not fill gaps from snippets or assumptions.
4. Update the affected topic or stage and the editorial `reviewed` date. Keep all
   referenced source IDs and local routes consistent.
5. Run the focused Life data/browser checks and `node scripts/check-content.js`.
   Frontend edits also require contrast checks, version stamping and responsive
   light/dark review under the repository's normal workflow.

## Integration with existing tools

Life links to the working Toolkit planners through public URLs:

- `toolkit.html?section=workbench&planner=moving-checklist`
- `toolkit.html?section=workbench&planner=four-city-budget`
- `toolkit.html?section=workbench&planner=document-deadlines`
- `toolkit.html?section=workbench&planner=career-tracker`

Those URLs contain only a tool choice. They do not carry checklist entries, health
information, finances, identity details, application notes or document contents.
The planners retain their existing explicit local-saving and export behaviour from
`docs/student-toolkit-v4.md`. Life does not create a second planner or change those
storage keys.

The Life guidance is public and does not collect remote input. Any page-level city
or topic choice is navigation context, not an account or a legal-status assessment.
Do not add personal-data fields, document uploads, clinical questionnaires, financial
records, submissions or remote tracking as part of content maintenance. Sensitive
records should go directly to the authorised official service using its process.
Users can follow existing Contact links deliberately; no message is sent by opening
the guidance.

The public JSON and page assets should be available through the existing service
worker so previously loaded guidance remains useful offline. External official
links still need a connection, and a cached source note is not a live recheck.

## Verification

The focused Life checks should establish schema/reference integrity, known city
slugs, existing Hub and Toolkit routes, accurate source-date labels, complete topic
and moving-stage navigation, empty/error recovery, keyboard access, narrow layouts,
both themes and public-only navigation state. Browser fixtures must remain fictional.
Do not send contact messages, submit university forms or include real personal records
in a test. Follow the repository's existing approval rules when committing or publishing.

## Page behaviour and maintenance

`student-life.html` and `mobility-guide.html` share `life.css` and `life.js`.
The city selector changes contextual guide links and official resource cards; it
retains all six shared topics. Student Life search matches topic advice/questions,
with an explicit empty result and reset. City and track identifiers can appear in
public shareable URLs; search text stays in memory. Browsing a track does not change
My track. Routes prepend shared Semester 1, read Semesters 2–3 from the track and
show the two Semester 4 thesis-host choices rather than an invented fixed itinerary.

Topic, stage and source hashes reveal their target and focus the relevant heading,
summary or source card. Source citations open the official-resource disclosure.
Print opens all details and includes filtered-out topics; after printing, disclosure
states are restored. Invalid or unavailable content/track files retain useful
fallback links and offer Retry; one failed file does not discard the other guide.

Shared menu entries also appear in the footer. Site-wide search indexes the public
JSON topics and moving stages. The service worker precaches both pages, their
styles/script and the guidance JSON; visited track data remains available through
its existing data cache. These pages introduce no storage keys or remote collection.

Run `npm run test:life`, `node scripts/check-contrast.js` and
`node scripts/stamp-versions.js --check`. The Life workflow runs the data/browser
checks and, after a main deployment, `node tests/life/live.test.js` as a read-only
check of exact published assets and both guides. Screenshot outputs are temporary
verification artefacts. The browser checks use fictional requests, verify actual
offline caching and exercise mobile themes, source/route failures, global search,
print restoration, keyboard navigation and local input isolation.
